The main differences between zkSNARKs and zkSTARKs lie in their cryptographic approaches, efficiency, and scalability. Here's a breakdown of the key differences:

### 1. Cryptographic Basis:
   - zkSNARKs (Zero-Knowledge Succinct Non-Interactive Argument of Knowledge):
     - zkSNARKs rely on pairing-based cryptography, specifically elliptic curve pairings. This requires trusted setups (a process where specific public parameters are generated that must remain secret to ensure security).
   - zkSTARKs (Zero-Knowledge Scalable Transparent Argument of Knowledge):
     - zkSTARKs, on the other hand, use hash functions instead of pairings, which do not require a trusted setup and are considered more secure in the long run.

### 2. Trusted Setup:
   - zkSNARKs: They require a trusted setup phase, where specific cryptographic parameters are generated. If this setup is compromised, it can jeopardize the security of the entire system. However, once set up, they offer very efficient proofs.
   - zkSTARKs: zkSTARKs are transparent and do not require any trusted setup. This is seen as an advantage because it reduces risks associated with the setup phase and makes the protocol more secure and decentralized.

### 3. Scalability and Efficiency:
   - zkSNARKs: Generally more efficient than zkSTARKs in terms of proof size and verification time, meaning they produce smaller proofs that are quick to verify.
   - zkSTARKs: zkSTARKs tend to be less efficient in terms of proof size and verification time but offer better scalability, especially for large computations or when scaling systems over time.

### 4. Proof Size:
   - zkSNARKs: zkSNARKs produce small proof sizes. This makes them more suitable for systems where bandwidth and storage are critical.
   - zkSTARKs: zkSTARKs generally produce larger proof sizes than zkSNARKs. This can make them less ideal in some cases but is compensated by their scalability and transparency.

### 5. Security Assumptions:
   - zkSNARKs: The security of zkSNARKs depends on the complexity of elliptic curve pairings and assumes the hardness of certain problems in cryptography (e.g., the Bilinear Diffie-Hellman problem).
   - zkSTARKs: zkSTARKs rely on the security of hash functions (like SHA256), which is believed to be more robust against quantum attacks and simpler in terms of cryptographic assumptions.

### 6. Quantum Resistance:
   - zkSNARKs: Because they rely on elliptic curve cryptography, zkSNARKs are vulnerable to quantum attacks (e.g., via Shor's algorithm).
   - zkSTARKs: zkSTARKs are considered quantum-resistant due to their use of hash functions, which are believed to be more resilient to quantum computing attacks.

### 7. Use Cases:
   - zkSNARKs: Often used in applications where efficiency and small proof size are critical, such as in privacy-preserving cryptocurrencies (e.g., Zcash).
   - zkSTARKs: Ideal for use cases that prioritize scalability, transparency, and long-term security, such as in decentralized computation and scalable blockchain protocols.

### Summary of Differences:

| Feature                 | zkSNARKs                              | zkSTARKs                          |
|-------------------------|---------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Cryptographic Basis  | Pairing-based cryptography           | Hash functions (no pairings)      |
| Trusted Setup        | Requires a trusted setup             | Transparent (no trusted setup)   |
| Proof Size           | Smaller proofs                       | Larger proofs                     |
| Scalability          | Less scalable                         | More scalable                     |
| Efficiency           | More efficient (smaller proofs)      | Less efficient (larger proofs)

## zkSNARKs and zkSTARKs

These are two of the most well-known types of zero-knowledge proofs, each with its own quirks and features.

- **zkSNARKs** 
    (Zero-Knowledge Succinct Non-Interactive Argument of Knowledge) are known for their compact proofs and fast verification. They're great for quick checks in cases like blockchain transactions. But they also need an additional initial trusted setup.
- **zkSTARKs** 
    (Zero-Knowledge Scalable Transparent Argument of Knowledge) are newer and focus on handling bigger and more complex proofs efficiently. They skip the trusted setup part, which makes them more transparent and theoretically safer in some ways. However, they tend to have larger proofs and can take longer to verify compared to zkSNARKs.

### Main differences

![](https://github.com/0xmetaschool/Learning-Projects/blob/main/assests_for_all/assets-for-zkverify-horizen/Lesson%202_%20Understanding%20Zero-knowledge%20Proofs/image2.webp?raw=true)

No need to worry if you’re unfamiliar with these definitions, Let’s simplify.

- **Trusted setup**
    - zkSNARKs: Requires a special phase at the start to create some initial settings securely, known as a "trusted setup.”
    - zkSTARKs: Do not require a trusted setup, promoting transparency and potentially improving security.
- **Proof sizes and speed**
    - zkSNARKs: Have smaller proof sizes and faster verification times, suitable for applications requiring quick responses.
    - zkSTARKs: Have larger proofs and take longer to verify, making them more suitable for scenarios prioritizing robustness over speed.
- **Succinct setup (Efficient initial configuration process)**
    - zkSNARKs: Setup time can vary but may take linear time or more depending on the implementation.
    - zkSTARKs: Usually get started quickly, in a way that grows slowly even as the system gets bigger ( Logarithmic setup time).
- **Scalability**
    - zkSNARKs: Efficient for certain scalability needs with verification time for proof-checking time growing slowly even as the system scales (Logarithmic verification time).
    - zkSTARKs: Provides quasi-linear proving time and logarithmic verification time i.e. proof-making time grows slowly and proof-checking time grows very slowly. It is suitable for handling larger and more complex proofs efficiently.
- **Applications**
    - zkSNARKs: Widely adopted in blockchain for their speed and efficiency in transaction verification.
    - zkSTARKs: Gaining traction in applications requiring high security and transparency, such as financial audits and privacy-preserving protocols.
